### 🎞️ NICHE FILE — not the greatest hits

> two directors, one frequency: rooms you can't leave, music you can't turn off

- The clocks in **In the Mood for Love** never agree with each other on purpose — nobody in a Wong Kar-wai frame is ever actually on time for their own life.
- **Chungking Express** runs on expiry dates — canned pineapple, love, a police badge number — as if feelings come with a sell-by stamp.
- **All About Lily Chou-Chou** never really shows you Lily. She's a forum thread, a shared silence, an "Ether" everyone logs into but nobody quite touches.
- The bullying, the "through-train" sequence — Iwai shoots cruelty in the same wide, drifting frames he shoots beauty in. No tonal warning. That's the actual gut-punch.
- Wong Kar-wai reportedly shoots without a finished script — actors improvising toward a mood he hasn't fully named yet. Fitting, for someone whose movies are all about people who can't name what they want either.

*if you know, you know. if you don't — go sit in a quiet room and watch one.*
